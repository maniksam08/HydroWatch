HYDRO-WATCH: WATER QUALITY GUIDELINES & INCIDENT RESPONSE SOP
Source: Australian Drinking Water Guidelines (ADWG) & Queensland EPP Water Guidelines

1. ENVIRONMENTAL VALUES (EVs) AND WATER QUALITY OBJECTIVES (WQOs)
The primary management goals for receiving waters are to protect and enhance aquatic ecosystems and ensure public safety. Aquatic ecosystem water quality objectives establish the baseline for normal operating conditions.

1.1 Normal Water Quality Parameters
- pH Levels: The normal pH range for freshwater rivers is between 6.5 and 8.5.
- Dissolved Oxygen (DO): Normal DO saturation should remain between 60% and 110%. In concentration, DO levels must remain above 5.0 mg/L to support healthy aquatic life.
- Total Phosphorus: Must remain less than 70 micrograms per litre (µg/L).
- Chlorophyll-a: Must remain less than 9 µg/L.
- Temperature: Normal river water temperature ranges between 15°C and 25°C.

2. ANOMALY THRESHOLDS & ENVIRONMENTAL INDICATORS
- Low pH (Acidic): A pH dropping below 6.0 indicates high acidity, frequently caused by industrial runoff, chemical spills, or acid sulfate soil disturbances.
- High pH (Alkaline): A pH rising above 8.5 can indicate excessive algal blooms or industrial discharge.
- Hypoxia (Low Oxygen): DO levels dropping below 5.0 mg/L cause stress to aquatic life. DO levels below 3.0 mg/L indicate extreme hypoxia and imminent fish kill events.
- Temperature Spikes: Rapid, unnatural increases in water temperature often correlate with thermal pollution, such as cooling water discharge from power plants or industrial facilities.

3. CRISIS MANAGEMENT & INCIDENT CLASSIFICATION (ADWG Element 6)
Incidents are classified into two categories based on their threat level to human health and environmental integrity.

3.1 Type 1 Incidents (Critical / High Consequence)
- Definition: Potentially serious incidents presenting significant human health risks or major environmental harm (e.g., Extreme hypoxia below 3.0 mg/L, major toxic spills, extreme pH drops).
- Reporting SOP: Must be reported immediately by direct voice contact to the Water Incident Coordinator and relevant environmental protection agencies (EPA). Direct voice contact must be followed up with a written notification/email within 24 hours.
- Action SOP: Immediate environmental team dispatch is required. The emergency response plan must be activated, and public warnings may need to be issued.

3.2 Type 2 Incidents (Warning / Low Consequence)
- Definition: Lesser incidents representing a low risk to human health and possible but localized environmental harm (e.g., pH dropping to 6.3, or DO dropping to 4.5 mg/L).
- Reporting SOP: Type 2 incidents do not require immediate emergency phone calls. They must be reported to the relevant agencies via email or notification system within 24 hours. If the incident occurs on a weekend or public holiday, it must be reported by 12:00 PM the next business day.
- Action SOP: Increased monitoring frequency is triggered to ensure the anomaly does not degrade into a Type 1 incident.

4. POST-INCIDENT PROTOCOLS
Following any Type 1 or Type 2 water quality incident, an investigation must occur. The investigation must document:
- The initiating cause of the anomaly.
- How the problem was first identified by the telemetry system.
- The most critical actions taken during the response.
- Review of the existing SOP to improve preparedness and planning for future incidents.
